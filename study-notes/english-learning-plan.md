## Learning sequence

### Phase 1 — English foundations

Rebuild the fundamentals needed for every section:

- sentence, subject, verb, and object
- noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, and adverb
- singular and plural
- basic tenses
- subject–verb agreement
- sentence meaning and vocabulary

### Phase 2 — Reading passage

Practise Section I, worth 20 marks:

- identify what `this`, `that`, `it`, `they`, and similar words refer to
- match information accurately
- decide True / False using evidence from the passage
- answer in complete sentences
- infer unfamiliar word meanings from context

### Phase 3 — Word forms and grammar

Practise the forms and rules needed for Sections III–V:

- change words between noun, verb, adjective, and adverb forms
- tense and subject–verb agreement
- articles
- active and passive voice
- direct and indirect speech
- questions and negatives
- conjunctions
- prepositions

### Phase 4 — Sentence transformation

Practise Section II without changing the original meaning:

- rewrite according to the instruction
- use equivalent expressions
- change affirmative, negative, and question forms when required
- check tense, subject, and meaning after rewriting

### Phase 5 — Note writing and essay writing

Practise Sections VI–VII:

- identify the topic and key points
- organise an introduction, body, and conclusion
- write short, clear sentences
- use paragraphs, punctuation, and correct spelling
- check grammar before finishing

### Phase 6 — Exam practice

- practise each section separately first
- complete mixed exercises after the basic rules are understood
- take timed 80-mark mock papers
- record repeated mistakes in one correction list
- review weak sections before taking the next mock paper

## Daily study routine

Use a 60–90 minute session when possible:

1. 15 minutes — vocabulary and word forms
2. 20 minutes — grammar
3. 20 minutes — reading passage
4. 20 minutes — writing or sentence transformation
5. 10 minutes — review and correction

## Teaching and checking method

### Mandatory section-opening lesson

Before practising any new exam section, teach the section itself thoroughly. Do not begin with isolated questions. The section-opening lesson must cover:

1. the section's purpose and marks
2. the meaning of its instructions and technical terms
3. the foundations the learner needs
4. every main question pattern expected in that section
5. clues for recognising which rule or answer form is required
6. a step-by-step answering method
7. fully worked model examples
8. common mistakes and a final checking method
9. an understanding check before exercises begin

### Question lesson sequence

After the section-opening lesson is understood, each question will follow this order:

1. define the words, symbols, and instructions
2. explain the rule from the beginning
3. show worked examples step by step
4. practise together
5. let the learner answer independently
6. correct mistakes and explain why they occurred
7. check understanding before moving on
