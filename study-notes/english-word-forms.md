## 1. What a word form is

A word family contains words with a shared basic meaning but different grammatical jobs.

| Job | Name | Burmese meaning | Example from `develop` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| action or state | Verb | ကြိယာ | develop |
| person, thing, place, or idea | Noun | နာမ် | development, developer |
| description of a noun | Adjective | နာမဝိသေသန | developed, developing |
| description of a verb, adjective, or adverb | Adverb | ကြိယာဝိသေသန | developmentally |

The task is not simply to change spelling. First identify the grammatical job of the blank, and then choose the form that gives the correct meaning.

## 2. The four main word classes

### Noun (N)

A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea. It can work as a subject or object.

Common clues:

- `a`, `an`, or `the` + noun phrase
- possessive word (`my`, `his`, `her`, `our`, `their`, `its`, `your`) + noun
- preposition + noun or gerund
- `this`, `that`, `these`, or `those` + noun
- noun + `of` + noun
- quantifier (`some`, `many`, `much`, `few`, `little`) + noun

Look at the whole noun phrase. In `a beautiful girl`, `beautiful` is an adjective because the noun `girl` follows it.

### Verb (V)

A verb expresses an action or state. A complete clause normally needs a verb.

Common clues:

- subject + verb + object
- `to` + V1
- modal (`can`, `could`, `may`, `might`, `must`, `shall`, `should`, `will`, `would`) + V1
- `do`, `does`, or `did` + V1
- `has`, `have`, or `had` + V3
- `let` + object + V1
- `go on`, `keep on`, or `carry on` + V-ing

After choosing a verb, also check tense, subject-verb agreement, and active/passive voice.

### Adjective (Adj)

An adjective describes a noun or pronoun.

Common clues:

- adjective + noun: `a healthy person`
- `be`, `look`, `seem`, `feel`, or `become` + adjective
- `very`, `quite`, `so`, or `too` + adjective when the phrase describes a noun or pronoun
- some V-ing and V3 forms can function as adjectives: `an interesting book`, `an interested student`

### Adverb (Adv)

An adverb describes a verb, adjective, or another adverb. Many adverbs end in `-ly`, but not all words ending in `-ly` are adverbs.

Common clues:

- verb + adverb: `She answered politely.`
- adverb + adjective: `very carefully prepared`
- adverb + adverb: `very quickly`

## 3. High-value position rules from the source

| Pattern around the blank | Likely required form |
| --- | --- |
| article + blank, with no noun after it | Noun |
| article + blank + noun | Adjective |
| possessive + blank | Noun |
| preposition + blank | Noun or V-ing |
| demonstrative + blank | Noun |
| subject + blank + object | Verb |
| blank + `of` + noun | Noun |
| `be` + blank | Adjective, V-ing, or V3; meaning decides |
| `very/quite/so/too` + blank describing a noun | Adjective |
| main verb + blank describing how | Adverb |
| `to`, modal, or `do/does/did` + blank | V1 |
| `has/have/had` + blank | V3 |
| `let` + object + blank | V1 |
| `keep/go/carry on` + blank | V-ing |
| `some/many/few` + blank | Noun; often a plural count noun |
| `much/little` + blank | Usually an uncountable noun |

These are clues, not automatic answers. Meaning and the full sentence must still be checked.

## 4. Common suffix clues

### Noun endings

- `-tion/-sion`: inform -> information; decide -> decision
- `-ment`: develop -> development; improve -> improvement
- `-ness`: happy -> happiness
- `-ity`: active -> activity
- `-ance/-ence`: perform -> performance; exist -> existence
- `-er/-or/-ist`: discover -> discoverer; act -> actor; physics -> physicist

### Adjective endings

- `-ful`: wonder -> wonderful
- `-less`: care -> careless
- `-ous`: danger -> dangerous; fame -> famous
- `-al`: commerce -> commercial
- `-ive`: effect -> effective
- `-able`: value -> valuable
- `-ic/-ical`: climate -> climatic; geography -> geographical
- `-y`: sun -> sunny

### Adverb endings

- adjective + `-ly`: careful -> carefully; complete -> completely
- spelling can change: happy -> happily; easy -> easily

### Verb endings

- `-en`: strength -> strengthen; soft -> soften
- `-ize/-ise`: modern -> modernize
- `-ify`: beauty -> beautify

Suffixes help recognition, but the sentence position decides the required word class.

## 5. Step-by-step answering method

1. Read the whole sentence before changing the bracketed word.
2. Find the subject, main verb, object, and important connectors.
3. Examine the words immediately before and after the blank.
4. Decide whether the blank needs N, V, Adj, or Adv.
5. Choose the correct member of the word family.
6. If it is a verb, check tense, agreement, and voice.
7. If it is a noun, check person/thing meaning and singular/plural form.
8. Check spelling changes.
9. Read the completed sentence again for grammar and meaning.

Use this compact exam notation above each blank when necessary:

`position clue -> required class -> correct form -> final check`

## 6. Worked model examples

### Example 1

`To be (health), we should eat (nutrition) and fresh fruit.`

- `be + ___` describes the person, so an adjective is needed: `health -> healthy`.
- `___ and fresh + fruit` uses two adjectives to describe `fruit`: `nutrition -> nutritious`.

Answer: `To be healthy, we should eat nutritious and fresh fruit.`

### Example 2

`They (grow) new plants every year. The plants need rain for their (grow).`

- `They + ___ + new plants` needs a present-tense verb: `grow`.
- `their + ___` needs a noun: `grow -> growth`.

Answer: `They grow new plants every year. The plants need rain for their growth.`

### Example 3

`Christopher Columbus (discover) America. He was a great (discover).`

- The completed past action needs V2: `discover -> discovered`.
- `a great + ___` names a person, so a person noun is needed: `discover -> discoverer`.

Answer: `Christopher Columbus discovered America. He was a great discoverer.`

### Example 4

`Education (strong) the foundation of a (civilize) society.`

- The singular subject `Education` needs a present-tense verb: `strong -> strengthen -> strengthens`.
- `a + ___ + society` needs an adjective: `civilize -> civilized`.

Answer: `Education strengthens the foundation of a civilized society.`

### Example 5

`The police have (complete) their (investigate) into the accident.`

- `have + ___` needs V3: `complete -> completed`.
- `their + ___` needs a noun: `investigate -> investigation`.

Answer: `The police have completed their investigation into the accident.`

## 7. Meaning distinctions that often cause mistakes

- `discoverer` = the person; `discovery` = the thing or event discovered
- `zoologist` = the person; `zoological` = adjective
- `physicist` = the person; `physics` = the subject
- `performer` = the person; `performance` = the act or show
- `producer` = the person or company; `product` = the thing; `production` = the process
- `interested` describes the person who feels interest; `interesting` describes the thing that causes interest

## 8. Common mistakes

1. Looking only at the bracketed word instead of the whole sentence.
2. Assuming that every blank after `a/an/the` is a noun; a following noun may make the blank an adjective.
3. Choosing the right word class but the wrong meaning, such as `discovery` instead of `discoverer`.
4. Forgetting tense or subject-verb agreement after deciding that a verb is needed.
5. Forgetting singular/plural forms after deciding that a noun is needed.
6. Adding `-ly` automatically without checking whether an adjective or adverb is required.
7. Memorising a suffix without learning spelling changes.
8. Trusting a word-family table without checking how the word functions in the sentence.

## 9. Final checking questions

Before submitting an answer, ask:

1. What job does this blank perform: N, V, Adj, or Adv?
2. Which words before and after the blank prove that choice?
3. Does the meaning require a person, thing, action, or description?
4. If it is a verb, is its tense and form correct?
5. If it is a noun, is singular/plural correct?
6. Is the spelling correct?
7. Does the completed sentence sound logical?
